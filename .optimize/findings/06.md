# Ticket 06 findings (GPU shaders), hunter report verbatim

Summary: one clearly worthwhile change, and it alters the picture. The nebula's 4.3-5 ms of GPU time at 1600x900 comes mostly from ray-marching at a higher resolution than SE uses. Only 06-4 (same-frame A/B shader) and 06-5 (screenshot) left pixels unchanged; each saves 0.1-0.5 ms. All timings: transparent-pass GPU time from paused runs at 1600x900.

Why base1 showed 16.7 ms: an external 60 fps cap, not GPU work. The median was exactly 16.66 ms while the transparent pass took 5.25 ms of GPU time (base2: 4.95). The port has no frame limiter, vsync was off and the monitor runs at 239 Hz. Most likely the driver or Windows throttled the window while it was out of focus. base1 neb_look switched between 5 ms and 16 ms frames, which fits a cap turning on and off.

What the nebula frame costs: only 1 volume is drawn (Skylias, full screen), so there's no overdraw. Each pixel runs up to 256 steps, and each step does 7 texture fetches, the 8-round spiral noise (32 sin/cos), 3 gas emissions and the dust maths. With the nebula shader returning early, the transparent pass drops from ~5.2 to 0.3 ms. After that the frame is CPU-bound at ~6 ms, so GPU savings only shorten frames at bigger windows (the monitor is 3440x1440) or once CPU work is cut. Multi-volume test: inside RN 8514-1463 with 11 more procedural volumes in view (12 in all), the transparent pass took 2.2 ms; 06-2 brought it to 2.05.
